# Code of conduct

Freshet is a small open-source project. Everyone who takes part — in issues, pull requests, reviews and
discussions — should be able to do so without harassment or hostility.

## What we expect

- Be kind and patient, especially with people who are new to the project or to programming.
- Assume good intent, and ask before you assume.
- Give feedback about the work, not the person, and take feedback the same way.
- Keep conversations on topic and in the open unless they need to be private.
- Respect people's time: search before you post, and say clearly what you need.

## What isn't acceptable

- Insults, slurs or demeaning comments about anyone's identity, background or ability.
- Harassment of any kind, including unwanted attention and repeated contact after someone has asked you
  to stop.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Sexual language or imagery, and threats of violence.
- Trolling, deliberately derailing conversations, or other sustained disruption.
- Using the project's spaces to share, or ask for, content you don't have the right to share.

## Where this applies

In all of the project's spaces on GitHub, and anywhere someone is speaking for the project.

## Reporting

If someone's behaviour worries you, tell the maintainer privately: open the repository's
**Security** tab, click **Report a vulnerability**, and say that the report is about conduct. It's the
only private channel GitHub offers for reaching a project's maintainer, and only you and the maintainer
can see it. You can also use GitHub's **Report content** option on any comment, which goes to GitHub's
own staff.

Reports are kept confidential, and the person who made one isn't named without their consent.

## What happens next

The maintainer will look at what happened and, depending on how serious it was, may:

1. ask the person privately to stop, as a warning;
2. hide or remove comments, commits or other contributions;
3. block the person from the project for a time, or permanently.
